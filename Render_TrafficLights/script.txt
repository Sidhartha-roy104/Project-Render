Ever wondered what's inside that grey metal box at every junction? It's called a traffic signal controller, basically a small computer deciding who moves and who waits. Traditionally, many signals ran on fixed schedules, switching between red, yellow and green at programmed intervals. But how does a signal know when traffic is building up? Some junctions use sensors buried in the road. Others use overhead cameras and computer vision to estimate queues. Adaptive systems then stretch or shorten green time based on demand.

Ever wondered how pressing a single key turns into a letter on your screen in milliseconds? Underneath every keycap sits a switch—either a rubber dome or a mechanical spring—waiting to close an electrical circuit. But wiring a hundred keys individually would create a tangled mess. Instead, keyboards use a grid of crossing rows and columns called a key matrix. When you press a key, it connects one specific row to one specific column. A tiny microcontroller constantly scans this grid, detects the exact coordinate, and translates it into a standard scan code. That code travels over USB or Bluetooth as a data packet, telling your operating system which character to draw. Which type of keyboard do you prefer—clicky mechanical or quiet membrane?
